Featured Artist: James Fairman (American 1826-1904)

Below, we’re sharing with you a first-look at a new James Fairman painting, before it appears on our gallery website. We hope you delight in the subtleties of its beauty.

Scottish-born James Fairman and his brother were brought to New York City when he was six years old. Encouraged in art when he was a child, at age 18 he took art classes at the National Academy of Design. Starting inauspiciously as a book binder for Harper’s publishing house in Manhattan, Fairman was eventually to become a landscape painter “without peer” as one newspaper of the day stated. He served in the Northern army during the American Civil War as a Colonel and he was to retain “Colonel” with his name throughout his life. It was only after the war that he fully pursued art as a career. Fairman did not embrace any particular “school” of painting although he spent a number of years in Dusseldorf, Germany; London, England, and Paris, France. Col. Fairman himself said that he belongs to “The Eclectic School”—“a class of artists that professes not to attach itself distinctly to the French or Dusseldorf, or Munich, or any other school, but having studied in all or several of the leading schools, aims at adopting good qualities from each.” During the 1870s, he visited the Holy Land and became the preeminent painter of Jerusalem in the United States. He traveled also throughout New England—Connecticut and Maine and the Catskill Mountains in New York State. Fairman did not belong to, or exhibit much at, the usual art venues of that time, preferring instead to promote his own work and obtain his own patrons—a task at which he was more successful than many of his peers. He did exhibit variously at the National Academy of Design, Brooklyn Art Association and the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts.

Finding Beauty in the Details

Over the years, we’ve had the pleasure of spending our days surrounded by beautiful original nineteenth-century paintings. Some have stayed with us for a time, and others have gone on to new homes, but so many have left a lasting impression. They continue to inspire the way we look at art and the care we put into our gallery, right down to its smallest details.

Often, it is a small detail in a painting that draws us back for another look. A delicate shift in color, a touch of light, or a few carefully placed brushstrokes can reveal something we hadn’t noticed before. Below, we’re sharing a first look at several new paintings with our Art Lovers’ Niche community, before they appear on our gallery website. We hope you’ll take a moment to linger over them and discover the details that speak to you.

Featured Artist: Albert Francis King

There is something especially rewarding about an artist who helps us look more closely at familiar things. Pittsburgh-born Albert Francis King (1854–1945) is remembered for his still-life paintings, with fruit among his recurring subjects. Largely self-taught, he also studied with Martin B. Leisser and painted landscapes at Scalp Level with Leisser and George Hetzel. His work gives us a wonderful opportunity to slow down and appreciate the care an artist can bring to a seemingly simple subject.

King’s Overturned Basket of Peaches is a lovely place to begin. Notice the gentle changes from yellow to red across the fruit, the leaves resting on the tabletop, and the light catching the slats of the basket. Each small detail contributes to the character of the whole painting. We invite you to let your eye wander through the arrangement and enjoy the color, texture and quiet beauty King found in these everyday objects.
